The tray, moved off the wallpaper

One row, C's minimal vocabulary: eight quiet presets · a divider · the custom-colour well · your own image, as the last tile in the same row. Below it, the current wallpaper in words. The four options differ only in how the tray is seated under the preview. All live — click, drag the well, drop an image on the last tile.

Theme

1 · Detached glass — the tray as a floating tool

C's frosted pill exactly, just landed below the preview instead of on it. It stays dark in both themes, so it reads as an instrument rather than as part of the page — and the preview above it is left completely clean.

2 · Attached footer — preview and tray are one object

The tray is the preview's footer: same width, square shoulders, no gap. Reads as a single “wallpaper” control and takes the least vertical room. Themed rather than glass, so it never fights the pane.

3 · Detached, themed — same shape, Settings' own colours

Geometry of 1, palette of the app: white on light, dark on dark. Quietest of the four and the most consistent with the rest of Settings; loses the “instrument” feel that made the glass version fun.

4 · Straddling the edge — half on, half off

The omnibar's shoulder trick: the pill sits on the preview's bottom border, half in each world, and the mini omnibar lifts to clear it. Keeps the “painting the desktop” feeling of C while leaving most of the wallpaper unobstructed.

Detail in all four
  • The custom colour well wears a conic rainbow ring so it's readable as “any colour” without a label, and it takes the selected ring when the current colour isn't a preset.
  • Your image is the last tile in the same row: dashed and empty until set, then it is the thumbnail (and takes the selected ring). Drop a file on it, or click.
  • The caption under the tray carries the words — Moss · #46534A or Your image — so no tile needs a label, and Reset lives on the caption line.
  • Eight presets fit one row at the pane's 560px max; a ninth would wrap, so the palette is capped at eight by the layout on purpose.
