Lighting states where a thing is. It can't lie twice.

A slot only makes sense for the bar, because the bar lives on the desktop. The sheet rises above every window — it cannot also be a hole in the wall behind them. So they share one material — same blur, same colour, same darkening — and take opposite lighting: the bar is cut in, the sheet stands proud. Same recipe, two truths.

The rule, completed

Three positions, three lightings

This is the whole system now, and each row is falsifiable — if a surface's lighting doesn't match where it actually sits in the stack, it is lying to the user. That is what the sunken sheet was doing.

The bench

Dial in the recipe

Depth does the readability work — brightness() pulls any backdrop into a known band, while colour pushes its hue back up so the material still reads as whatever is behind it. The two floors are separate on purpose: the bar sits over wallpaper, but the sheet sits over windows, which can be pure white, so it needs a heavier neutral floor. Click the bar to raise the sheet — the windows dim and recede behind it, exactly as the shell already does.

One recipe, five walls

These update live with the bench. The riskiest text in the dock is here — the 10px section label and the 10.5px placeholder — over a blown-out wallpaper, a saturated one, a high-frequency noisy one, and a near-black one where a shadowed recess has the least to work with.

Readability · the sheet

The harder case: over a window

The sheet's backdrop is not the wallpaper — it is whatever window it covers, already dimmed to brightness(.65) by the shell. A white document is the worst of these, and it is the one the bar's own floor was never tuned for.

What holds

Light text, always. The bar is in shade and the sheet is a dark panel, so the ink never flips between themes — one readability answer, and no backdrop can invert it.

Depth at or below 0.65 keeps the 10px label safe on all five walls, including the pale one.

What to watch

Drop the sheet's floor below ~0.14 and a white window starts bleeding through it as pale grey — the label goes first. That slider is the one with a real minimum.

Near-black wallpaper is the bar's limit: it can't get much darker than its surroundings, so the cut reads only through the rim and lip. Keep both.

Cost

Two backdrop-filter surfaces, the same count as today — the rule just splits in two, since the bar and the sheet no longer share a lighting.

The banner floats above the windows too, so it takes the sheet's lighting, not the bar's.

What actually changes

The shared rule at app.css:694 splits: .frn-dock-bar becomes the cut, .frn-dock-sheet and .frn-dock-banner become the raised panel. Geometry, width calculation and animations are untouched. The readout is the rule — it tracks the bench.
