Lighting says where a thing is. Movement says what you can press.

Everything in this document falls out of those two sentences — the button, the dock bar, the sheet above it, and the one thing Appearance has to expose. The dock's look is a recipe of five numbers, but a person does not want five numbers. They want four stops on one axis, shown on their own wallpaper, and the readable one already chosen.

1

Lighting states position

Raised means a control. Cut in means a container. Floating means it is above the windows. A surface whose lighting disagrees with where it sits is lying.

2

Movement states control

The 1.5px press-drop belongs to the button and to nothing else. If it moves when you touch it, it does something. The dock never moves; picks in Settings never move.

Rule two · the only thing that moves

The button — Keycap

What Aqua got right was that it looked pressable. Keep that and throw the gloss away: a flat fill, one solid edge underneath, and a press that drops 1.5px and eats the edge. The edge is a single flat colour, so it stays honest in dark mode, where gradients go muddy.

This replaces the gradient in packages/design/mac.css. Everything stays in :where(), so it remains a zero-specificity default that any app rule beats. Press them.

The rule it carries

The press-drop is reserved. Nothing else in the system may translate on :active — not a dock icon, not a wallpaper swatch, not a material tile. That is what makes it mean something.

Focus keeps the edge and adds a ring, so a keyboard user sees the same object a mouse user does.

Where it costs

It is taller than today's button: 5px 13px against 2px 11px. Dense rows — the Accounts pane, a card footer — take data-size="sm", which is the old metrics with the new edge.

1.5px of real motion, so it needs the reduced-motion guard that is in the delta below.

Rule one · two positions, two lightings

The dock — The Slot

The bar lives on the desktop, so it is cut into it: shadow inside the top edge, a lit lip along the bottom, and no outer shadow, because a hole does not float. The sheet rises above every window, so it cannot also be a hole in the wall behind them: no inset shadow, a uniform hairline, a real drop shadow. One material, opposite lighting.

Click the bar to raise the sheet — the windows dim and recede behind it, exactly as the shell already does at app.css:546. Change the material and the wallpaper to see what each one survives.

What holds

Light ink, always. The bar is in shade and the sheet is a dark panel, so the text never flips between themes. One readability answer, and no wallpaper can invert it.

The field is a deeper cut inside the bar, not a lighter pill on top of it — depth accumulates in one direction.

What it replaces

The shared rule at app.css:653 splits in two. Six --glass-* variables and the sheen gradient go; the bar keeps the recess, the sheet and the banner take the float.

Geometry, the width calculation and every animation are untouched.

Cost

Two backdrop-filter surfaces — the same count as today. On Solid it is zero: the filter is removed rather than set to a no-op, so no compositing layer is created at all.

The banner floats above the windows too, so it takes the sheet's lighting, not the bar's.

The recipe, published

Four stops on one axis

The bench has five sliders. The axis a person actually has an opinion about is how much of the wallpaper survives, and there are four useful answers along it. Every stop obeys the two limits the bench found, which is what makes a preset worth shipping and a slider not: brightness stays at or below 0.65, past which a near-white wallpaper washes the bar out, and the sheet's floor never drops under 0.18, below which a white window bleeds through it as pale grey.

Clear and Frosted are retuned from the bench — their depth was 0.72 and 0.74, both over the line. Pulled to 0.64 and 0.65 they keep their character, which was never brightness: Clear is the sharp one (10px blur, 210% saturation), Frosted is the milky one (28px blur, 130% saturation). They differ in kind, not only in amount.

The bar, every material against every wall

The riskiest text in the dock is here: a 9px placeholder over a blown-out wallpaper, a saturated one, a high-frequency noisy one, and a near-black one where a shadowed recess has the least to work with. Solid is the row that never changes — which is the point of having it.

Smoked is the default

It is the recipe the bench was tuned to. A device that has never opened Settings shows Smoked, and the Dock card rings Smoked — absence and choice look the same, because they are.

Solid is not a downgrade

It is the accessibility answer and the low-power answer in one word. It is also forced. When the system asks for reduced transparency the shell uses Solid whatever the file says, and the pane says so under the tiles.

Pale is the honest weak spot

Look at that row. On a near-white wallpaper Clear and Frosted put the placeholder — deliberately the lowest-contrast text in the dock — at its limit; the icons and the labels hold. Smoked is the row that does not struggle, which is a good part of why it is the default.

Night is the other end: the bar cannot get much darker than its surroundings, so the cut reads only through the rim and the lip. Both stay in the rule for exactly that case.

apps/settings · Appearance

One stage, two instruments

The pane already simulates the desktop, because the question a wallpaper answers is not "is this a nice colour" but "can I still read my work on it". That is the same question the dock's material answers, and the answer to one depends on the other — so they share one picture. The simulation comes out of the Wallpaper card and becomes the pane's own stage; two cards below it drive the same image.

Working mockup, whole pane, real size. Pick a wallpaper, hover a material to preview it, click to commit, and click the dock in the stage to open the sheet.

Theme

The tile is the thing

Each sample is a 1:1 crop of your own dock — real 62px bar, real 40px icon, over the wallpaper you just picked. Not a shrunken picture of one: at quarter size a 28px blur and a 10px blur look identical, and the tile would be lying about the only thing it exists to show.

Hover previews, click commits

Pointing at a tile repaints the stage; leaving puts it back. Nothing is written until you click. The pane already does exactly this for a dragged colour, so it is the pane's own established rule, not a new one.

Picks get a ring, commands get an edge

A swatch and a material tile are choices: they take a selection ring and never move. Reset is a command: it is a keycap, and it drops when pressed. Rule two, enforced inside one card.

No Reset on the Dock card

Wallpaper has one because "no wallpaper" is a real, different look. There is no "no dock" — the default is Smoked, and Smoked is already a tile. A second way to reach the same state would only be a second thing to explain.

The caption does the teaching

One line under the row, following whichever tile you are pointing at: the name, then what it costs you. It replaces the help text nobody reads and the tooltip nobody finds.

It disappears on hosts without a dock

The pane already handles CAPABILITY_UNAVAILABLE for the wallpaper — the Mac app hits it today. The Dock card rides the same flag: no dock, no card, no error.

The delta

What actually changes

The choice is one word on the Volume, exactly as a wallpaper is one file. The shell reads it, puts it on the dock as an attribute, and CSS holds the recipes — so no arithmetic crosses the boundary, and adding a fifth material later is four numbers in a stylesheet.

The whole mechanism

Six properties, two lighting rules, one attribute, one file. There is no dock-material module.

/* packages/design/design.css — the material */
[data-material] { --dock-ground: 8 11 16; --dock-scale: 1; }
[data-material="clear"]   { --dock-blur: 10px; --dock-sat: 210%; --dock-bright: .64; --dock-tint: .08; --dock-sheet-tint: .26; }
[data-material="frosted"] { --dock-blur: 28px; --dock-sat: 130%; --dock-bright: .65; --dock-tint: .16; --dock-sheet-tint: .30; }
[data-material="smoked"]  { --dock-blur: 20px; --dock-sat: 200%; --dock-bright: .58; --dock-tint: .10; --dock-sheet-tint: .20; }
[data-material="solid"]   { --dock-ground: 22 26 31; --dock-blur: 0px; --dock-sat: 100%; --dock-bright: 1; --dock-tint: 1; --dock-sheet-tint: 1; }

/* --dock-scale is for PREVIEWS only: a blur radius is a length and has to
   follow the scale of the picture. Saturation, depth and the floors are
   ratios and must not — that is why they are not multiplied anywhere. */
.ds-slot, .ds-raised {
  backdrop-filter: blur(calc(var(--dock-blur) * var(--dock-scale))) saturate(var(--dock-sat)) brightness(var(--dock-bright));
  color: #f2f5f9;
}
.ds-slot {   /* cut in: no outer shadow, a hole doesn't float */
  background: rgb(var(--dock-ground) / var(--dock-tint));
  border: 1px solid rgb(0 0 0 / .34);
  border-bottom-color: rgb(255 255 255 / .16);
  box-shadow: inset 0 4px 8px -2px rgb(0 0 0 / .5), inset 0 1px 0 rgb(0 0 0 / .3), inset 0 -1px 0 rgb(255 255 255 / .12);
}
.ds-raised { /* above the windows: no inset shadow at all */
  background: rgb(var(--dock-ground) / var(--dock-sheet-tint));
  border: 1px solid rgb(255 255 255 / .14);
  box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgb(0 0 0 / .26), 0 22px 50px rgb(0 0 0 / .46);
}
[data-material="solid"] :is(.ds-slot, .ds-raised) { backdrop-filter: none; }
@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce) { /* forced, whatever the file says */
  :is(.ds-slot, .ds-raised) { backdrop-filter: none; background: rgb(22 26 31); }
}

Calls made, and the ones turned down

Each of these was a real fork; the reason is the part worth keeping.

Presets, not sliders The bench stays a bench. 3-slot-bench.html is where the numbers get tuned; Settings ships the results. Five sliders named "Neutral floor" would let anyone build an unreadable dock, and every stop above is a point that was checked against five wallpapers. Publishing the sliders would publish the failures with them.
Four, and the fourth is Solid Niche and Normalized were the same material at two depths — one of them had to go. Solid earns the slot the other way: it is the only stop that answers reduced transparency, weak GPUs and a battery, and it makes the axis complete rather than merely long.
One word on the Volume Not reducer state — that replicates, and a fact about a file would then have a second, disagreeing copy. Not JSON either: the file holds the word smoked, and an unreadable one reads as unset. It follows you to every device for the same reason your wallpaper does.
Which apps are pinned: not here Real, and a different feature. It belongs to the dock itself — drag to reorder, right-click to unpin — where the thing you are editing is under the pointer. Putting a list of apps in Settings would be building the worse version of it first.
Light/dark: still the system's call The bench had a theme switch to test with; it is not a setting. The shell follows the OS, and the dock's ink is light in both themes anyway — which is precisely the property that makes one material recipe enough.
