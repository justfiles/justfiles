frieren · shell design spec — variant 1a, revised

The Expanding
Dock

A search field, the apps, and Frieren — one row. Click the field and the apps step aside so it can fill the bar; type to filter them, or type anything else and it goes to Frieren. The bar's outline never moves.

What changed

Two edits that fix the transition problem

The earlier morph turned a dock into a field, and it failed because nothing survived the change — there was no object to follow and no place for the trigger to live. Both edits below solve exactly that.

Edit 1

Frieren joins the dock

The agent stops floating in its own corner and becomes a dock item: same size as an app icon, same row, in its own section at the end — where macOS puts Trash and Downloads. One face, one place, and it is the drawer's handle.

Edit 2

The field expands; it doesn't replace

Click the search box and the app icons clear out so it can run the width of the bar. The field is present before, during and after — it is the thing you clicked, and it just got bigger. That is an interaction people already know from every mobile search bar.

The design

Three states, one outline

The bar's box — its width, height, position and corner radius — is identical in all three states. Nothing about the container animates, which is what keeps the expansion calm rather than theatrical.

Slab

52px tall, 14px radius, 9px padding, 8px gaps. Frosted like today's pill, 20px off the screen's bottom edge.

Field

132×34 at rest, 9px radius, inset so it reads as a well rather than a button. Placeholder is one word: Search.

Icons

34px at 24% radius, in a stable order you arrange — never re-sorted. A running app carries a dot beneath it.

Row cap

Width is 203 + 42n for n icons. So n = clamp(3, ⌊(available − 203) ÷ 42⌋, 10), where available is about 70% of the desktop — narrower once the drawer opens.

Sections

One hairline rule cuts the row into search · apps · Frieren, so the agent reads as its own thing rather than the last app.

The transition

The icons don't vanish — they move up

This is the detail that makes the expansion teach itself. As the field grows, the icons don't fade into nothing; they rise into the sheet, where they land as a labelled grid with room for every installed app. The question "where did my apps go?" answers itself on the first use, because you watch them arrive.

Reverse on Esc or a click outside, and they come back down. One gesture, learned once.

One rule underneath all of it: the icons are for pointing and the field is for typing, and neither gesture ever triggers the other. Clicking an app never expands the bar; typing never launches anything without ↩. That separation is why this version doesn't need to be taught.

Edge cases

The parts that need a decision

Notifications need a new home

Today the banner rides in the placeholder slot of a 640px field. A 132px field can't carry one. So the banner becomes a transient row above the bar — the same place the sheet appears, borrowing a grammar that's already there. info auto-recedes; error and question stay until answered. Unread history badges Frieren's face.

Presence lives on the face

The glow can't live on the slab any more — a rotating ring around a row of app icons reads as an error, not as thinking. It moves to Frieren's face, which is now a fixed 34px circle and a perfect host: a ring for working, a breathing pulse for needs‑you. The row is capped by the width available

How many icons show is derived from the viewport, not a fixed number. Take what's left after the field, the rule and Frieren, and fit whole icons into it — clamped to never fewer than three and never more than about ten, past which you're scanning rather than pointing.

The row truncates from the end; it never re-orders, so the apps you put first stay where your hand expects them. Anything hidden is one keystroke away in the field and always present in the grid. Scrolling the row horizontally is a possible refinement — deliberately not now.

The drawer takes width from the bar

The bar is centred on the desktop, and the drawer narrows the desktop rather than covering it. So opening the drawer re-centres the bar and tightens the cap — the row simply shows fewer icons.

No special case is needed: the drawer is just another way the available width changes, and the rule above already handles it. There is also no empty state to design — the built-ins are materialised on every boot, so even an anonymous account opens with a full row.

Decided

Settled

Grid order

A grid, ranked by recency, capped. Keeping the grid shape but ranking it by last use — and cutting it at twelve — lands on recents without needing a second list. Everything past the cap is one keystroke away, which is the point of having the field right there.

After asking

Collapse, and move focus into the drawer. One input at a time. The drawer owns the conversation and owns the caret; the bar goes back to being a bar.

App Store

Out of the bar, last tile in the grid. iOS doesn't put the App Store in the dock, because the dock is for what you use daily — but it's always one swipe away. Same logic. It also buys back 51px, and it lets the row mean one clean thing: your apps.

Row length

Derived from the viewport, clamped. Fit whole icons into the space left over, three at minimum and about ten at most. The row truncates from the end rather than re-ordering, so position stays learnable.

Presence

On Frieren's face, not the slab. A rotating ring around a row of app icons reads as an error, and pulsing the whole bar while you're trying to launch something is hostile. The face is a fixed 34px circle and a natural host.

Running apps

Focus the window. No cycling, no minimise-on-second-click. One window per app today, so there is nothing to cycle through, and the simple rule is the one worth keeping when that changes.

Two things that follow from these

Both are consequences of the answers above rather than new questions, but they need a decision before this can be built.

Consequence

Held app updates lose their badge. The store icon carried it, and the store is now behind a click. Nothing else surfaces a held update, so it has to become a notification — an info in the banner row, routing to the store tile. That's the machinery this spec already has; it just needs wiring.

Consequence

Rank the grid on open, then freeze it. If recency re-sorts live, icons move under the pointer between glancing and clicking. Compute the order when the sheet opens and hold it for that engagement — the grid may be ranked, but it must never shuffle while you're looking at it.

Later

Ranking the row by how active an app is. Worth trying, but note it trades positional constancy for relevance — the opposite trade from the grid, and on the surface where constancy matters most. It wants a prototype before it wants a spec.

One thing worth naming: the bar and the grid now sort differently on purpose. The bar is a stable arrangement you control — muscle memory, never re-ordered. The grid is ranked by recency — for finding your way back. Same icons, two jobs, two orderings; getting this backwards would make the bar unusable.
